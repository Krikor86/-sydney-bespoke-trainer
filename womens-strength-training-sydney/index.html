The Training Focus

Build Muscle.
Build Capability.

This isn't a generic “toning” routine. We use progressively challenging resistance training, thoughtful technique and individual programming to pursue measurable strength and body-composition goals.

01

Glutes, Legs and Lower-Body Strength

If stronger glutes and legs are a priority, I'll design your program around that goal while still training your whole body. Appropriate squats, split squats, hinges, step-ups and machine exercises can help develop strength and muscle through a useful range of motion.

02

Muscle Growth and Body Composition

Progressive overload, appropriate weekly training volume and a clear plan for increasing loads or repetitions form the foundation. I'll also guide you through practical protein and nutrition targets where relevant to your goal.

03

Bone Strength and Healthy Ageing

Resistance training and appropriate weight-bearing activity can support bone strength as well as muscle and balance. Your program will account for experience, recovery and individual risk; it won't promise to treat osteoporosis or guarantee a particular bone-density result.

04

Strength Through Midlife

Whether you're starting in your thirties, returning to training or navigating perimenopause and menopause, we'll adjust exercise selection and progression around your capabilities and recovery rather than assuming everyone needs the same routine.

For further evidence-based information on exercise and bone health, see the Royal Osteoporosis Society guidance. If you've been diagnosed with osteoporosis or have had a fragility fracture, seek advice from your treating clinician before beginning a new program.

Common Questions

Women's Strength Training.
Questions Answered.

Do I need previous weight-training experience?

No. I start by assessing your movement, experience and goals, then choose exercises and loads appropriate for you. You can begin as a complete novice or build on years of training.

Can strength training help maintain bone density?

Appropriately programmed resistance and weight-bearing exercise can help maintain or improve bone strength. Results vary, and training is not a substitute for medical assessment or treatment of osteoporosis.

Will the program focus on my glutes and legs?

If developing your lower body is a priority, yes. We can emphasise glutes and legs within a balanced strength program, using exercises such as squats, split squats, hinges and step-ups that suit your movement and equipment.

Is training suitable during perimenopause or menopause?

Yes. Resistance training can be an important part of maintaining muscle, strength and function through midlife. I adjust the program to your goals, energy, recovery and any relevant health considerations.

Where are sessions held and who coaches me?

You train one-to-one with me, Greg Kafalian, inside Snap Fitness, a commercial gym at 35 Clarence Street in Sydney CBD near Wynyard Station. This is not a private studio.
